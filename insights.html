Passive Investment Income and the Small Business Deduction Grind

The federal government enacted strict measures to prevent companies from deferring excess active income in private corporations to earn passive investment income under the preferential small business tax rates.

Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs) pay corporate income taxes on small business income at 9% federally plus 3.2% in Ontario (12.2% combined). The small business limit eligible for this rate is $500,000 annually. Above $500,000, the general corporate rate jumps to 26.5% in Ontario.

Under the passive income rules, every $1 of adjusted aggregate investment income (AAII) earned in a corporation (and associated group) above $50,000 grinds down the active business limit by $5, completely eliminating the small business rate once passive income hits $150,000.

Income Sprinkling Rules & Tax on Split Income (TOSI)

The Tax on Split Income (TOSI) rules represent one of the most significant structural changes to Canadian private corporate taxation in decades. The federal government’s stated objective is to eliminate the tax advantages of distributing dividend income to related family members who have not made a sufficient contribution to the family enterprise.

When TOSI applies, income received by family members (dividends, certain shareholder benefits, capital gains on share sales) is subjected to the highest marginal personal tax rate (over 53.53% in Ontario) with zero personal tax credits permitted.

Key Excluded Business Exceptions We Evaluate:

  • Excluded Business Test: Family members actively engaged on a regular, continuous, and substantial basis (averaging at least 20 hours per week during the relevant year or any five prior years).
  • Excluded Shares Test: Adult family members aged 25+ holding shares representing at least 10% of votes and value in a non-service operating company.
  • Reasonable Return Evaluation: Documenting capital contributed, work performed, and risks assumed by family participants.

Proper share capitalization, dividend documentation, and employment records are required to withstand CRA audits. Our firm routinely audits corporate share classes to ensure family businesses remain safely within statutory exemptions.

Carbon Tax & Ontario Climate Action Incentives

The federal carbon pollution pricing system imposes a levy on fossil fuel distributors and industrial emitters, accompanied by direct household rebate payments (the Canada Carbon Rebate, formerly CAIP).

For small businesses and transportation-heavy enterprises across the Greater Toronto Area, escalating fuel charge adjustments impact operational margins and supply chain costs. We assist commercial clients with tracking energy input credits, clean economy investment tax credits (ITCs), and compliance filings.
