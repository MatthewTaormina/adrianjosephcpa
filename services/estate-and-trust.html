TEP Credentialed Expertise

Protecting What You Have Built for Future Generations

Estate planning involves planning for how an individual’s assets will be preserved, managed, and distributed after death. It also considers the management of properties and financial obligations if an individual becomes incapacitated.

Assets that comprise an estate include family businesses, private corporation shares, houses, recreation properties, investments, pensions, life insurance policies, and debt. Our aim—whilst preserving family wealth—is to preserve family unity among beneficiaries.

As a recognized tax specialist with the global Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners (STEP), Adrian Joseph works in close harmony with your estate lawyers and financial advisors to design an enduring plan that stands the test of time.

What a Comprehensive Estate Plan Involves

We listen to your wishes and family priorities to construct a resilient roadmap covering all legal and financial dimensions.

📜

1. Will & Document Review

Reviewing existing wills to recommend structural enhancements that reduce taxes, prevent disputes, and coordinate smoothly with corporate articles and shareholder agreements.

🛡️

2. Trust Structuring

Evaluating whether lifetime trusts (inter vivos) or testamentary trusts created upon death best protect beneficiaries, manage tax brackets, and safeguard family wealth.

⚖️

3. Executor & Guardian Selection

Advising on naming suitable executors, alternate trustees, and legal guardians for dependent children, ensuring administrative burdens are manageable.

🎯

4. Beneficiary Alignment

Auditing named beneficiaries on registered plans (RRSPs, RRIFs, TFSAs) and life insurance policies to prevent accidental tax traps or probate exposure.

🤝

5. Powers of Attorney (POA)

Ensuring enduring powers of attorney are established to legally direct financial assets, investments, and personal care if incapacity occurs.

🕊️

6. Legacy & Philanthropy

Designing charitable bequests, donor-advised funds, and funeral arrangements that express your values while generating substantial charitable tax credits.

Executor Support Services

Post-Mortem Tax Administration & CRA Clearance

When a loved one passes away, the executor assumes personal fiduciary liability for all tax filings. We relieve executors of this burden through systematic, expert administration.

Tax Filings & Elective Returns

  • Terminal (Final) T1 Tax Return: Reporting all income up to the date of death and calculating deemed disposition of capital assets and registered funds.
  • Rights or Things Separate Return (Sec. 70(2)): Utilizing elective filings to report earned but unpaid income, claiming duplicate basic personal tax deductions.
  • T3 Trust Information & Estate Returns: Managing income earned by the estate during administration prior to asset distribution.

CRA Clearance Certificate (Sec. 159(2))

An executor who distributes estate assets without obtaining an official CRA Clearance Certificate remains personally liable for any unpaid taxes owed by the deceased up to the value of the distributed property.

We prepare and submit the TX19 form and assemble all required corroborating notices of assessment to obtain clearance certificates swiftly.

Periodic Review of Your Estate Plan

Life does not stand still. Marriage, separation, the birth of grandchildren, the acquisition of new business interests, or changes in Canadian tax laws (such as trust reporting rules and capital gains inclusions) mean an estate plan should be systematically revisited every 3 to 5 years.
